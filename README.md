# Analysis of five years of crowdsourced U.S. Amazon purchase histories with user demographics

This repository provides an analysis of this dataset that encompasses five years of Amazon purchase histories from U.S. consumers, collected through crowdsourcing methods. Each record includes information such as product titles, prices, quantities, and shipping states. Importantly, these transactional details are linked with demographic data from survey responses, covering aspects like age, gender, income, education, race, and household size. This linkage enables multifaceted analyses of purchasing behaviors across different population segments. More detailed information and the original files used for analysis can be found [HERE](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/YGLYDY).
## Code

This repository contains 2 of 3 of the .csv dataset files, 1 .malloynb file where my code and analysis can be viewed, and 2 PDF documents that provide additional background on the survey conducted. 
  [`fields.csv`](fields.csv), contains the "Survey ResponseID" and the associated question asked to customers.
  [`survey.csv`](survey.csv), contains the answers from the demographic questions asked to customers. Some of the demographic groups include: age, ethnicity, gender, income, Amazon useage, and various health and habit related questions. 
  "amazon-purchases.csv", includes the following: order date, purchase price per unit, quantity, shipping address, address state, title, product code, purchase category, and survey response ID. It is not included in this repository due to the size of the file, but can we viewed and downloaded for personal analysis at the link in the above informational section. 
  
  [`FavoritoFinal-Amazon.malloynb`](FavoritoFinal-Amazon.malloynb), is where my analysis of the collected Amazon data was performed and is displayed.

  ## Project Background

  The initiative behind this dataset stems from a need to understand consumer purchasing patterns in the context of demographic factors. By leveraging crowdsourced data collection, the project amassed a substantial volume of real-world purchase information, which, when combined with self-reported demographic details, provides a unique lens through which to examine e-commerce trends and behaviors.
  
  ## Motivation

  Analyzing this dataset can yield valuable insights into how various demographic groups engage with e-commerce platforms like Amazon. Potential areas of exploration include:

      - Market Segmentation: Identifying purchasing trends among different age groups, income brackets, or educational levels.

      - Product Preferences: Understanding which demographics are more inclined toward certain product categories.

      - Behavioral Patterns: Examining how factors like household size or state of residence influence purchasing decisions.

  Such analyses can inform targeted marketing strategies, product development, and policy-making aimed at addressing consumer needs and promoting equitable access to goods and services.

  ## Summary of Findings

  The analysis connects Amazon purchase data with demographic and behavioral survey responses to explore patterns in consumer activity. It reveals how factors like age, income, education, geography, and personal habits influence what people buy, how often they shop, and how much they spend. The work also examines attitudes toward data privacy in relation to actual purchasing behavior, offering insights into the intersection of lifestyle, consumption, and personal values.

## Description of how other can build off this work

Future researchers can expand this analysis by incorporating time-based trends, joining additional datasets for richer context, clustering consumers by behavioral traits, or developing predictive models. This foundation supports further exploration into how personal and societal factors shape online purchasing and can inform strategies in public policy, marketing, and social science research.
